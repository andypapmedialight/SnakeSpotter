          If you spot a snake

          Safety tips

          Snakes along Edgars Creek want to be left alone. Several species here are highly venomous. Do not handle any snake — including ones that look small or dead.

          Give it space

          • Stop. Keep still for a moment so you don't startle it.
          • Back away slowly. Don't step over it, kick it, or run past it.
          • Don't try to catch, kill, or move it. That's how most bites happen.
          • Don't corner it or block its path to water or cover.
          • Warn people nearby, especially children. Keep everyone several metres back.
          • Watch feet and hands near long grass, rocks, logs, and the water's edge.
          • Wear closed shoes on the trail. Don't reach into hollows or under debris.

          Keep pets safe

          • Call dogs in at once and put them on a lead. Don't let them sniff, bark at, or attack the snake.
          • Keep cats indoors when snakes are turning up along the creek.
          • If a pet is bitten, keep it as still and quiet as you can and go to a vet immediately — don't wait for signs. Call ahead if you can.
          • Don't try to catch the snake for the vet. A photo from a safe distance is enough if it has already gone.

          If a person is bitten

          Call 000 immediately. Treat every bite as serious, even if it looks minor.

          • Keep the person still and calm. Don't walk them around.
          • Apply a pressure immobilisation bandage over the bite and up the limb if you know how. Ambulance will talk you through it.
          • Don't wash, cut, suck, or ice the bite, and don't use a tourniquet.
          • Don't try to catch the snake. Tell the ambulance what it looked like if you saw it.

          General public advice for this creek, not a substitute for emergency services or a vet. People: 000. Pets: a vet, straight away.
